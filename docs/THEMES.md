# Look and themes

User decisions on the scrollbar, the editor's box, themes, the look and the theme editor, from `/rs:steer` on 2026-11-04 and 2026-11-06 (moved here unchanged from [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md#look-and-themes-2026-11-04) to keep that file within the tools' read limit), and on the looks on 2026-11-19.

User decisions from `/rs:steer` on 2026-11-04 ([the task list](TASKS.md) holds the order; the theme editor is an exception to building beyond Scramjet only once the daily driver is solid):

- **Scrollbar.** Scramjet's: the rightmost column, shown while the conversation is taller than the view, clickable and draggable. It is a visual aid to where the reader is.
- **The editor's box holds only the prompt and its completion list.** Notes and messages go to the status row above it.
- **Themes.** Exact colors (24-bit, with a 256-color fallback), and a built-in "Terminal colors" theme that keeps today's 16 terminal colors. A theme may set the main background; otherwise the terminal's own background is used, asked of the terminal so that contrast can be scored. The built-in `scramjet-dark`, `scramjet-light` and `pi-light` paint their own background (user decision, 2026-11-16, unlike Scramjet 1), so they look alike in every terminal; "Terminal colors" keeps the terminal's. Underneath, a theme is a palette of main colors plus every role, each following one of them or holding its own exact value. The default theme follows the terminal's background as Scramjet's does: `scramjet-dark` on a dark or unknown one, `pi-light` on a light one; a chosen theme wins (2026-11-06). At start the full screen asks the terminal only for its background, as Scramjet does; its text color and 16 colors when the theme list opens (2026-11-06).
- **A distinct look**: a visual language for reasoning, tool calls, prompts and replies. Picked at the steer of 2026-11-06 from captured screens of one scripted session: `panels` is the default look and `header` the default banner; the other looks and banners stay offered in `/settings`. Panels follows Scramjet's spacing of boxes, without copying Scramjet's interface: a tinted padding row above a call's header, under it and at the bottom, as user messages have; one left edge for all text; a failed call's output in its normal color on the error background. Its own marks stay: the outcome on the header, the time there, the tool's name before its arguments, `›` on prompts. Polishing it means comparing it with Scramjet on the same scripted session, judging each difference.
- **Every look developed to panels' level** (user decision, `/rs:steer` of 2026-11-19, second). Panels is detailed and polished, from Pi and Scramjet; `classic`, `rails` and `margin` signal much less (screens of `scripts/screens/sample.json` that day: classic shows every call with the same dim `●`, running, done or failed, a failure only by red output, no time, and its prompt's `>` is the editor's; rails' state is a small glyph and the rail's color, with no rhythm of spacing and no rail on replies; margin's state is the label's color only, its column narrows output so that it wraps, and failed output is red text; the call-state roles `toolPendingBg`, `toolSuccessBg`, `toolErrorBg` are panels' alone). So:
  - **A list of signals** every look shows distinctly, panels being the benchmark, written here first: a call running, done or failed, and how long it took; folded long output; prompts, reasoning, replies and where a turn starts; questions, subagents, error and retry notices; selection and copying; each in a dark theme, a light one and "Terminal colors" (16 colors). Each look meets every signal by its own means (background, rail, glyph, label, border, weight, spacing), at least as clearly as panels.
  - **The three others reworked,** each with a clearer identity: classic stays low on chrome but its mark takes the state's color and the time shows (near Claude Code's, not copied); rails gets a rail that says the state plainly and one beside replies; margin becomes a log-like column of labels in the state's colors without costing output its width.
  - **New looks inspired from outside,** built for the user to pick from: `cards` (rounded borders around a call, colored by its state, its icon and time on the edges, after Gemini CLI); `outline` (`•` actions with `└` output under them, a bold verb, a "worked for" line between turns, after Codex); `timeline` (one spine down the conversation with a node per event, `○` running, `●` done, `✗` failed, as `git log --graph` draws); `mono` (no hue but for failures; state by weight, glyphs and space, so it holds without color).
  - The loop decides the details itself, each a `Choice:` trailer, and new theme roles get their rows in the theme editor. A scripted session covering every signal is captured in each look and each kind of theme as a gallery; the user picks at a steer which looks stay, are renamed or dropped. None is removed without the user's say; panels stays the default.

  **The signals**, as panels shows them on 2.269.0 (`scripts/gallery.py` plays `scripts/screens/signals.json` in each look under `scramjet-dark`, `pi-light` and `terminal-colors`; the shot that shows each is in brackets):

  1. *A call running* [running, subagent]: its header on `toolPendingBg`, `○` in the warning color at the right; the status row's spinner says `Running`.
  2. *A call done* [end]: on `toolSuccessBg`, `✓` in the success color.
  3. *A call failed* [end]: on `toolErrorBg`, `✗` in the error color; its output keeps its normal color, so it stays readable.
  4. *How long it took* [running, end]: muted, before the glyph at the right, for a shell command of a second or more (other tools show none); whole seconds counting up while it runs.
  5. *The call itself*: the tool's name bold, then what it was called with; a file tool's path in the accent, a read's lines in the warning color; a command's later lines under its first.
  6. *Folded long output* [end]: `… (N earlier lines, Ctrl+O to expand)` muted, inside the call's block.
  7. *Prompts, and where a turn starts* [end]: a band of `userMessageBg` with a padding row above and below, `›` in the accent, the text bold.
  8. *Reasoning* [end]: `thinkingText`, italic, at the replies' left edge.
  9. *Replies* [end]: markdown at one left edge shared with the blocks' text, on no band.
  10. *Questions* [question, end]: while asked, the editor's box holds the options; once answered, the asking call's block shows the question and `→` before the answer.
  11. *Subagents* [subagent, end]: inside the call's block, each child's name bold with its state (`⏳` running, `✓` done), its latest action after `→`, then its usage line.
  12. *Error and retry notices* [retry, end]: in the warning color, outside any block, at the replies' edge; the status row counts the retry down.
  13. *Selection and copying* [selected]: selected text reversed; a copy leaves out every look's gutters, rails and padding (`text::FROM`, `UNTIL`).
  14. *Each in a dark theme, a light one and 16 colors*: what the backgrounds carry must still be told apart in "Terminal colors".

  Panels' own gaps, fixed with the others: a running call showed no time until it ended (since 2.275.0 its whole seconds count up at its heading's right); notices, and the session's own messages, started at column 0, off the one left edge, until 2.274.0: they now start at the replies' edge in every look but classic, the banner staying at column 0 with the status row and the editor. (In "Terminal colors" it had no bands at all, so a call's state was its glyph alone and a prompt only its `›`; since 2.273.0 a band the theme gives no background is drawn as a `▎` edge down the block's left in its state's color, the accent's for prompts.)
- **Tinted cards, a candidate to replace panels as the default** (user decision, `/rs:steer` of 2026-11-19, third). Cards is the closest look to panels in quality; panels' backgrounds say a call's state more clearly. So a new look, beside `cards` (which stays as it is), puts cards' rounded borders around a background that carries the state: inside each call's card `toolPendingBg`, `toolSuccessBg` or `toolErrorBg`, inside a prompt's `userMessageBg`. The fill carries the state, so a done card's border is neutral (`borderMuted`) and only running and failed cards keep a border in their state's color. Where the theme paints no backgrounds ("Terminal colors"), it shows as `cards` does today, its colored border carrying the state. The loop names it and decides the details, each a `Choice:` trailer, and adds it to the gallery. Panels stays the default until the user, having seen it beside panels and cards in the gallery, says it replaces panels. Built as `tinted` (2.280.0).
- **A `scramjet-light` theme** (user decision, `/rs:steer` of 2026-11-19, third): built-in, the light companion of `scramjet-dark` (its own palette at readable contrast on a light background, painting its own background as the built-ins do). It is offered beside `pi-light`, which stays the default on a light terminal until the user says `scramjet-light` replaces it.
- **A theme editor in `/settings`** that lets users with no artistic background discover a theme they like, quickly, over a live preview of a sample session showing every UI element: choose 1–5 main colors and a harmony that fits that number (monochrome, complementary, analogous, triadic, split-complementary, square, rectangle…, each described in one plain line); roll a whole theme in it (text, dim text, borders and block backgrounds derived at readable contrast and tinted toward the main colors, every role filled) until it is close; lock colors you like, so a roll changes only the others and locked colors anchor the harmony; set any element to an exact color or reset it to follow its main color, all roles one view away; bump toward the harmony in small steps, or contrast up or down. Undo and redo cover rolls and bumps. From 2026-11-06: choosing another harmony keeps the colors, changing only the score and what a bump aims at; Alt+Up and Alt+Down reorder the main colors; a theme started from another stays recognizably itself through contrast and background changes (its colors shift, not jump to newly derived ones); the main background's lightness steps lighter or darker than the derived one, keeping its tint from the first main color.

  Scores read at a glance (2026-11-06): each score's quality shown by color (red bad to green good, or a background), with labels saying what they measure, capitalized alike, not a bare number or words after each; the loop tries forms on screen and picks one, with `Choice:` trailers. Harmony is scored on hue only, in a perceptual space where lightness and chroma stay fixed as the hue turns; each template hue is a sector, the template's rotation fitted to the palette or anchored by locked colors, each color counting by its chroma (grays barely count); repeated bumps converge to a perfect score. A contrast guard keeps every text/background pair within a band neither too faint nor too harsh, nudging lightness as little as needed after each step, or the color skips it; contrast bumps change only lightness. Colors with a meaning (errors, success, warnings, diff added/removed) keep their hue ranges, pulled toward the harmony only within them; each can be set by hand or have its range switched off. The color wheel defining "opposite" (artist's or perceptual) and the contrast formula (WCAG 2's ratio or APCA) are the loop's choices, judged by the randomizer's results, with `Choice:` trailers.
